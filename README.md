## Abstract

The minimal number of negations needed to compute a Boolean function is
governed by Markov's decrease invariant d(f): circuits need ⌈log₂(d(f)+1)⌉
negations (Markov 1958), and formulas need exactly d(f) (Morizumi, ICALP 2009;
preliminary version arXiv:0811.0699). We show that in the four-valued
De Morgan (dual-rail) setting, the decrease invariant acquires a
*cohomological address*. The Klein four-group V₄ = ⟨σ, P⟩ generated by the
negation σ and the rail-swap P acts on the four-valued lattice; its character
group Ext¹ ≅ Hom(V₄, 𝔽₂) ≅ 𝔽₂² has exactly three nonzero vectors, and we
prove that the coordinate χ_σ is the *unique* character that prices negation:
χ_σ(γ) equals the negation cost of γ for every γ ∈ V₄, while the remaining
characters fail in both directions (the monotone rail-swap P costs nothing yet
flips the resolved face; the involution σP is cost-1 yet face-trivial). Our
main theorem then reads the full invariant as an iterated extension:
**d(f) equals the minimal length of a nested χ_σ-tower** — a ladder
F₀ ⊆ F₁ ⊆ ⋯ starting at the monotone clone in which each rung adjoins exactly
one negation inside a monotone context. The upper bound is given by an
explicit uniform witness h(x) = 1, ¬f(x), 0 according as the local decrease
potential ω(x) is ≥ k, intermediate, or 0; the lower bound is an alternation
argument. The degree is fixed at one throughout: the tower index is a *level*,
not a cohomological degree. All results are machine-verified in
dependency-minimal Lean 4 (kernel axioms `propext`, `Quot.sound` only; the
finite character tables are axiom-free; no `native_decide`, no `sorry`).

## 1. Background

Markov (1958) determined the inversion complexity of Boolean functions in
circuits: ⌈log₂(d(f)+1)⌉ NOT gates, where d(f) is the maximal number of
1→0 descents of f along an increasing chain. Morizumi (ICALP 2009;
preliminary arXiv:0811.0699) proved the formula-model law: exactly d(f)
negations. His upper-bound construction is the parallel decomposition
f₁ ∨ (f₂ ∧ ¬f_t); our nested single-peel normal form (§3) strengthens this
shape even in the Boolean case. For multi-valued logic,
Kochergin–Mikhailovich extended the *circuit* law — their k-valued bounds
remain logarithmic (⌈log₂(d+1)⌉ for Post negation, ⌈log_k(d+1)⌉ for
Łukasiewicz); no formula-exact multi-valued law appears in the literature.
Negation-limited models more broadly: Santha–Wilson (constant depth),
Sung–Tanaka (bounded depth), Amano–Maruoka (clique lower bound),
Guo–Komargodski (formula structure).

The present note contributes (i) the exact-equality theorem in the
four-valued monotone-order setting (building on the author's companion
preprint [10], Theorem 5.3), and (ii) the cohomological reading, which
appears to be new: a literature sweep found no prior cohomological or
deformation-theoretic treatment of bilattice negation cost.

## 2. The receptor and the three faces

Dual-rail lattice D4 = {0,1}²; σ(a,b) = (1−a,1−b) (order-reversing negation),
P(a,b) = (b,a) (monotone rail-swap); V₄ = ⟨σ,P⟩. H¹(V₄;𝔽₂) = Hom(V₄,𝔽₂) = 𝔽₂²
with three nonzero characters: χ_σ+χ_P (the flip of the resolved face
R = {(1,0),(0,1)}), χ_σ (the endpoint axis), χ_P.

**Proposition (gate typing).** For γ ∈ V₄, the negation cost ν(γ) ∈ {0,1}
equals χ_σ(γ); the face-flip character mis-prices in both directions,
witnessed by P (flip 1, cost 0) and σP (flip 0, cost 1).
*Machine-checked, axiom-free `decide` (`gate_table`, `chiSigma_eq_not_kMono`).*

## 3. Main theorem

For nonconstant f : Rⁿ → R let d(f) be the chain-decrease. Define the nested
tower: T₀ = the monotone clone; T_{k+1} = { g(x, ¬h(x)) : g monotone,
h ∈ T_k }.

**Theorem.** f ∈ T_k ⟺ d(f) ≤ k; hence d(f) = minimal tower length, and each
rung consumes exactly one χ_σ-twist.

*Upper bound*: with ω(x) the maximal decrease along chains ending at x, the
uniform witness h := 1 on {ω ≥ k}, ¬f on {1 ≤ ω ≤ k−1}, 0 on {ω = 0} covers
every monotone violation and satisfies d(h) = k−1 exactly (a three-zone
budget argument; the top-zone cap is essential — the naive ¬f∧[ω≥1] witness
fails already at k = 1). *Lower bound*: one adjoined negation raises d by at
most one (ascent/descent alternation along chains; valid over any finite
poset). Every statement is machine-verified in the Lean artifact in `lean/`.

## 4. Level, not degree

The tower index k is a filtration *level* at fixed cohomological degree 1;
no identification with any higher-degree or higher-level operator theory is
claimed — the scope is kept deliberately minimal.
